Joining two songs

Each test plays the last 12 seconds of one song straight into the first 12 seconds of the next. Pick an album whose songs run into each other (a live album is best), and the first of the two songs.

  1. Run each test with the screen on and listen at the join: is there a gap, a click, or a clean join?
  2. Run each again, and lock the phone as soon as it starts: does the second song still come in?

Round 2. Round 1 showed that plain players survive a locked phone and the mixer doesn't. Tests 5 to 8 are new: please run those, each with the screen on and then locked.

1. Two players, the second started when the first ends

The simplest way. Expect a small gap.

2. Two players, the second started just before the first ends

The second is started a fraction of a second early, by a timer.

3. Two players through a mixer, crossfaded over 6 seconds

The songs overlap and fade into each other. This is the route crossfade and volume levelling would need on an iPhone.

4. Both songs decoded in memory, joined to the sample

An exact join. Takes a moment to load before it starts.

5. Two players, the second started just early (corrected)

Test 2 again, with the time left measured properly. Listen for a gap or an overlap at the join.

6. Crossfade, with the mixer's sound sent out through a player

Test 3 again, but the mixed sound is played by an ordinary player, which a locked phone may leave alone.

7. Exact join, with the sound sent out through a player

Test 4 again, the same way.

8. Exact join, with a silent player running beside it

Test 4 again, with a second, silent player kept going, which sometimes keeps a locked phone from stopping the mixer.

When you're done, tell Claude for tests 5 to 8: what you heard at the join with the screen on, and whether it kept playing with the phone locked. A screenshot of the grey box helps too.
